Design & Development Process

Q: What is your typical design process?
A: My vibe-first design process is human-centered and problem-solving driven. It follows 8 structured steps: (1) Discovery & Define Problem — understand business needs and user goals, (2) Research & Define Solution — competitor analysis, user personas, and journey mapping, (3) Gather Content — collect assets, plan sitemap, optimize copy, (4) Design System & Components — build reusable UI components with accessibility, (5) Wireframing — define page structure and user flow, (6) Visual Design in Figma — apply styling and create high-fidelity designs, (7) Prototyping in Figma — simulate the website experience with interactive flows, (8) Developer Handoff — prepare design assets and collaborate during the build phase.

Q: What is your typical development process?
A: My development process follows 8 efficient steps: (1) Style Guide Setup — typography, color tokens, and naming conventions, (2) Client-First & Variables — global spacing, scalable folder structure, (3) Components & Layouts — modular navbar, CTAs, and responsive components, (4) Wireframes to Layout — translate wireframes into functional layouts, (5) Visual Design Implementation — pixel-perfect styles based on Figma, (6) Animation & Interactions — hover, click, and scroll-based effects using GSAP/Framer Motion, (7) SEO & Speed Optimization — meta tags, image compression, Core Web Vitals, (8) Domain & Launch — custom domain, SSL, final QA, documentation, and post-launch support.

Q: How do you measure website quality and performance?
A: My core value proposition is hitting top-tier metrics: 100 Performance, 100 Accessibility, 100 Best Practices, and 100 SEO on Google PageSpeed Insights and Core Web Vitals. Every site I deliver is tested against these benchmarks before going live.

Q: How do you ensure website accessibility?

Accessibility is not an afterthought — it is built into my design system from Step 1.

Technical Deep-Dives

Q: What is your approach to responsive design?
A: I follow a mobile-first responsive design philosophy. Every layout is built to adapt seamlessly from the smallest phone screen to the largest desktop monitor. I use CSS Grid, Flexbox, and fluid typography/spacing to ensure a consistent experience across all devices. I test on multiple screen sizes and real devices before deployment.

Q: What is your approach to website animations?
A: I believe animations should be purposeful, not decorative. I use GSAP, Framer Motion, and CSS transitions to create smooth hover effects, click interactions, scroll-triggered reveals, and typewriter effects. All animations are performance-optimized and include accessible motion safety checks (respecting the `prefers-reduced-motion` system preference).

Q: How do you handle content management for clients?
A: I set up user-friendly CMS solutions that empower clients to update their own content without touching code. Whether it is Webflow CMS, WordPress with Elementor, or a custom Supabase backend, I ensure clients can manage blog posts, portfolio items, product listings, and general page content independently. I provide documentation and training during handoff.

Q: What is Client-First naming convention?
A: Client-First is a professional naming and folder structure methodology I follow in Webflow projects. It creates a clear, maintainable, and scalable class structure using consistent naming conventions (like folder_component-name), global spacing variables, and organized wrapper logic. This approach ensures any future developer — or the client themselves — can easily manage and extend the site.

Q: Do you implement structured data and SEO schemas?
A: Yes. I implement JSON-LD structured data (Schema.org) for every site I build. This includes Person/Organization schemas, product schemas for eCommerce, FAQ schemas, breadcrumb schemas, and any other relevant structured data. This helps search engines better understand and display your content in rich search results.

Q: How do you handle browser compatibility?
A: I test all my builds across major browsers (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge) and devices (desktop, tablet, mobile). I follow web standards, use progressive enhancement techniques, and ensure graceful degradation for older browsers when necessary. Final QA testing is a mandatory step before any launch.
